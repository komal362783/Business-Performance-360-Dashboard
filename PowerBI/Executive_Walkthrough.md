# Executive Walkthrough
## Business Performance 360° Dashboard — Presented to the GlobalMart Leadership Team

*The following is written as a presentation script — the kind of narrative a Data
Analyst would actually deliver walking the CEO and executive team through this
dashboard for the first time.*

---

**"Good morning. Over the last few weeks we've built a single source of truth for
how GlobalMart is performing — across every country, category, channel, and rep.
Let me walk you through it."**

### Opening on the Executive Overview

"This is the page you'll open every Monday morning. Six numbers, no scrolling.

We did **$112.6 million** in revenue this reporting period, at a **47.2% profit
margin** — that's **$53.1 million** in profit. We processed just over **101,800
orders**, averaging **$1,106** each.

The line chart in the middle is the story of the business since launch — steady,
consistent growth from month one. I want to flag one thing before you ask about
the shaded region on the right: that's the most recent few months of data, and
we've marked them as an *incomplete reporting window* rather than let them imply
a decline. The same way you wouldn't panic about this month's numbers on the 3rd
day of the month, we don't want this dashboard to cry wolf on partial data. The
complete, comparable trend is the solid line — and it's a good one.

On the right, Apparel and Sports & Outdoors are neck-and-neck at the top of our
category mix, together driving over a quarter of total revenue. And down in the
corner, that gauge — we're sitting at **105.5% of target** on average across our
30 regions. We're not just hitting the plan, we're beating it."

### Moving to Sales Analysis

"Click over to Sales, and this is where I'd point our channel strategy
conversation. Online is our biggest channel — **$51 million**, about 45% of
revenue — followed by In-Store at 30%. Wholesale and Marketplace are smaller but
healthy.

The rep leaderboard at the bottom isn't just a scoreboard — it's a coaching
tool. Patricia Jones leads at **$4.5M** in revenue; the data bars make it easy
for a sales manager to see at a glance who's above average and who might need
support. That's a real, actionable use of this page, not just a vanity metric."

### Customer Analysis — a candid caveat

"Here's where I want to be direct with you, because I'd rather flag a data
nuance myself than have someone else catch it. The repeat-customer rate on this
page reads at **99.99%** — essentially everyone. That number is real, but it's
inflated by how densely our order volume is packed relative to our customer
base — about 12-13 orders per customer on average in this dataset. In a live
system with a lower order-to-customer ratio, I'd expect this number to sit
meaningfully lower and be a genuinely useful retention signal. I've documented
this clearly so nobody makes a strategic call off a misleading number — that's
exactly the kind of thing I'd rather catch in the room than in a board deck.

What I *do* trust on this page: the segment mix — Consumer customers drive 56%
of revenue — and the top-15 customer list, which is a legitimate account-based
marketing target list."

### Product Analysis — where to double down, where to cut

"This page answers 'what should we stock more of, and what should we
discontinue.' TV Stands and our Vacuum Cleaner lead the top-10 list. On the
flip side, the bottom-10 products collectively generated under half a percent
of total revenue — that's your delisting shortlist, not a hunch, a number."

### Regional Analysis — the target conversation

"This is the page for the regional VP conversation. Germany-Hesse and
UK-England are running at 122% and 116% of target respectively — ask what
they're doing right and see if it travels. On the other end, a handful of
regions are sitting just under 100% — not alarming, but worth a check-in
before next quarter's planning."

### Profit Analysis — margin health

"Revenue is a vanity metric without this page. We're at a healthy 47% blended
margin, and the distribution chart shows most orders cluster in a strong
40-60% margin band — but there's a visible tail of low-margin transactions
worth investigating, likely tied to our deepest discount tiers."

### Returns Dashboard — the quiet cost

"Returns cost us **$5.8 million** in refunds — about 5.2% of revenue. 'Not as
Described' is our leading return reason, which usually points to a product
listing or photography problem, not a quality problem — a much cheaper fix.
Sports & Outdoors has our highest return rate at 6.3%; I'd loop in that
category team."

### Forecast Dashboard — closing on the future

"Last page, and the one I'd leave you with. Based on the complete-window trend,
we're looking at continued double-digit growth into next quarter. I've built
this with a scenario table, not a single number, because I don't want to hand
you false precision — here's what next month looks like at 0%, 5%, 10%, and
15% growth assumptions, so you can pressure-test planning conversations
against a range, not a guess."

### Closing

"That's the tour. Every number on every page ties back to the same clean,
audited dataset — the same one we validated through SQL, Excel, and Python
before it ever reached Power BI. If a number here doesn't match what your team
sees elsewhere, that's a signal worth chasing down, and I'd want to know."

---

*End of walkthrough script.*
